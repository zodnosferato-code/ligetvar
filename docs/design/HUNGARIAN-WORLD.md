# Hungarian history and living folklore

Authoring canon established 29 October 2026 under the user's request to expand Hungarian tales, traditions, historical influences and character backstories. This supplements STORY-REVIEW, CHARACTER-VOICES and CASTLE-GEOGRAPHY; it does not replace their plot, voices or map. Runtime coverage is recorded only in TRACKER. Two playable meetings introduce this layer; the full history below is an authoring foundation, not a claim that every detail can already be encountered.

## Three kinds of truth

**Documented history** belongs to named sources below. **Fictional estate history** belongs to Ligetvár alone. **Stories told within the fiction** can disagree and are not proof of supernatural events. Do not pass an invented anecdote off as a collected Hungarian tale, a historical quotation or a real person's biography. Real figures inspire interests and institutions; no cast member secretly descends from them. No real historical person is made the vampire or a practitioner of the game's magic.

The setting remains a contemporary residential campus in fictional Transdanubian wooded foothills. National folklore collections can reach it through books, family travel and students; a Székely collection is not evidence that its every custom originated beside Békás Lake. Regional, religious and family variants matter. Characters can enjoy a custom, dislike it, adapt it or sit it out. No participation implies romance, faith or a gender role.

## People carry history differently

These are new fictional personal details compatible with existing roles, not replacements for appearance, attraction or relationship decisions.

- **Boglárka:** Her grandmother grew Luca wheat and hummed over dishes. As a thirteen-year-old, discovering Bartók and Kodály's collecting inspired an overearnest attempt to record her. Now she wants to remember what the singer enjoyed, not merely acquire a specimen. Kriza János's *Vadrózsák* (1863) [3] and Benedek Elek's collections [4] give her different printed voices to compare; she does not treat either as a transcript of every village. She once drew extra branches on an égig érő fa because she did not want the story to end. Family affection and curiosity sit alongside her loud-music taste and independent article.
- **Rebeka:** Her father kept a postcard of Nádasdladány. The spotless image helped create her childhood idea that grand houses lived outside ordinary life. Campus laundry and crowded cupboards have overturned that fantasy. She wants images that leave workers and students in the frame. Historical architecture becomes an argument about seeing, not an imposed photography lecture or a claim that she visited the real castle with Márton.
- **Gréta:** She used to admire the name on a school foundation; she now wonders who provided chairs, heat and time. The fictional Ilona card and real Teleki example matter because practical labour is easily erased. She can admire it without volunteering to organize every gathering. Her desire for leisure survives the historical parallel.
- **Márton:** Early in his teaching here, he grandly labelled a balance exercise a táltos trial without a source. A colleague challenged the label; he kept the exercise and abandoned the claim. This is an embarrassing memory, not a new magical technique or a repudiation of the genuine mystical discipline he knows. Hungarian belief is not a decorative certificate for his authority.
- **Virág — authoring foundation, not new runtime dialogue:** Her interest in institutional access can include noticing whose name a commemorative plaque omits. Teleki's example is a question she might ask about present opportunity, never evidence for her corruption cases. Keep Szabad Hullám fictional, her investigation independent, and her existing adult reporter history unchanged. A future separate records-room scene can contrast the public heritage brochure with ordinary maintenance costs without involving the main women or inventing a historical scandal.
- **Soma — authoring foundation, not new runtime dialogue:** As an assistant he has routine familiarity with the estate's maintenance history and can answer a mundane question about a replaced fitting. No invented aristocratic bloodline, occult ancestor, sinister folklore lecture or unique knowledge of a curse. His ambition remains his own; history does not excuse or reveal his later choices.
- **Lilla and ordinary campus residents:** They can prefer practical forms of company and decline festive noise. No disappearance victim becomes a sacrificial maiden from a ballad; recovery and missing-time evidence retain their existing meanings.

## Tales and everyday atmosphere

**Égig érő fa.** The tree joining worlds is a documented Hungarian folktale motif [5], not exclusive to Hungary and not proof of an actual portal. In the Luca scene, the player adds a resting bench to an original comic drawing with Boglárka. The characters identify their version as their own. A later illustrated bookmark is a possible callback only if that choice was witnessed.

**The bench that waited — original Ligetvár story seed, not collected folklore.** A gardener promises to put a bench where two quarrelling walkers can see the same view. Each insists on a different shore. The gardener leaves the bench beside the path; the walkers finally sit while arguing where to carry it. This small campus invention can support affection and disagreement without a tragic lover's ghost or a new lake location. It has not been scripted yet.

**Táltos and the workshop.** Do not equate a táltos with a generic wizard, vampire hunter or every modern movement teacher. Márton's anecdote is about a bad name, not an origin revelation. Any future supernatural mechanic requires a separate plot/engine brief.

**Songs and material culture.** Bowls, grain, cherries, folded paper, rubbed thresholds and photographs give hands something to do. Songs can be described or newly improvised; do not copy a modern arrangement or assume a recording is reusable. Refer to Bartók/Kodály's actual collecting [6] without inventing a field visit here. No extra audio or image assets are shipped by writing these scenes.

## Calendar and two refined scene briefs

The game presents seven-day weeks, not daily travel. Dates below are occasion dates; narration says preparations or the week of the occasion rather than pretending every action happens on the displayed Thursday. Ordinary slots and required chapter order are unchanged.

| Scene | Calendar, admission and place | Beats, choices and exit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `folklore_luca` | Week 11,10–16 December 2026; daytime; winter; shared_1 completed. Library, east wing. Cast: Márton, Boglárka, Rebeka, Gréta. No relationship or investigation knowledge required. | Preparation for 13 December; grandmother's wheat; invented 1892 reading-room card and real Teleki contrast; choose wheat sorting, a comic sky-tree drawing or departure. Personal memories are spoken now, not shared off-screen history. Leave via entrance gallery. Grain does not grow instantly. |
| `folklore_ivan` | Week 39,24–30 June 2027; nighttime; summer; shared_1 completed. Courtyard arcade, canonical rainy week. Same cast; Luca attendance unnecessary. | Szent Iván customs described as reported traditions [8]; a contemporary battery-lantern adaptation instead of an outdoor fire; Márton's teaching memory; choose an improvised tune, cherries/postcard conversation or departure. Stack chairs and part towards separate buildings. No implied kiss, marriage prediction, fire jumping or lake teleport. |

Each has at most three choices at its single menu, distinct activity branches and a complete early goodbye. One normal slot is consumed, including departure. Only the chosen event memory is committed on completion. No bonds, evidence, money, commitments or new ability are granted. Journals are deliberately valid for all branches and do not claim participation in an unchosen activity. Each event expires after its week and cannot block the main story. New scene IDs leave existing saves/cursors intact; passed occasions stay passed.

Further traditional calendar material can include family-specific Easter sprinkling, farsang and harvest gatherings, but these are unscripted expansion candidates requiring sourced briefs and separate small batches. Do not relabel Halloween as an ancient Hungarian custom, transplant Mohács-specific busójárás as the estate's native practice, or add compulsory romantic contact in the name of tradition.
